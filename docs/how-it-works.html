Your AI operating system

How it works

A folder of plain text files that Claude reads before it starts. It holds who you are, what you work on and how you like things done, so every session starts with context instead of from zero.

The map

The router sits in the middle and points at everything else. Claude reads it first, then opens only the page a task needs.

The parts

Every part is a file or a folder you can open and change. Nothing is hidden in an app.

The router

CLAUDE.md

The first page Claude reads in every session. It says where things live and how to behave, and points at the brain for the facts. Keeping it short is what keeps sessions fast.

Like the index page of a binder.

The brain

brain/

Plain markdown notes, linked to each other: about you, one page per project with its open tasks, the decisions you made and why, and a wiki of what you learn from videos and articles. Obsidian shows it as pages and a graph.

Like a notebook a good assistant keeps about the person they work for.

Skills

.claude/skills/

Written recipes for jobs that repeat: /writing drafts in your voice, /deadline puts a hand-in on your calendar, /jury stress-tests a decision. When you correct a result, the recipe gets fixed so the mistake does not come back.

Like recipe cards: the same dish, done right every time.

Rules

.claude/rules/

How Claude works here: verify before saying done, never send or spend without your yes, hand bulk reading to helper agents, write without the usual AI tells. Your own agreements from setup sit on top.

Like the house rules taped inside a cupboard door.

Hooks

scripts/hooks/

Small scripts that run at fixed moments without anyone asking: list what is waiting in your inboxes at the start of a session, stamp the date on edited notes, warn when two windows edit one file, back everything up to your private copy at the end.

Like alarms that ring by themselves.

Cadence and routines

cadence.md · scripts/routines/

The list of recurring work with when it last ran and when it is due. Claude reads it at the start of each session. If you allow it, two cloud routines e-mail you: a morning brief and a weekly review, even when your computer is off.

Like a wall calendar of routines, plus a note slipped under the door each morning.

A normal day

What happens on its own, and what you do.

  1. Morning e-mailWhat is due today, your calendar, deadlines this week, open tasks. Sent by a cloud routine that read your private backup.
  2. You open a session in this folderHooks run. Claude reads the router, checks the cadence list and both inboxes, and tells you in one line what needs you.
  3. You ask for somethingClaude opens only the brain pages that task needs, runs the matching skill, and checks the result before calling it done.
  4. You correct itThe correction becomes a feedback memory, and the skill that made the mistake gets patched.
  5. You close the sessionClaude updates the pages the work touched. A hook checks the links and backs everything up to your private GitHub copy.
  6. End of the weekThe weekly review e-mail: what got done, what went stale, the system's score, and one improvement worth making next.

How a correction sticks

The system gets better because your corrections are written down where the next session will read them.
