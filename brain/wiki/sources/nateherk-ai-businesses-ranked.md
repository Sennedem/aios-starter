# Nate Herk: "Every AI Business Ranked from Worst to Best"

YouTube, 14:40, Nate Herk | AI Automation, published 2026-11-05: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O3g_m2i2IGU.

Nate ranks 13 AI business models from worst (13) to best (1), scoring each from 1 to 5 on three axes: **Technical** (a lower score means more skill required), **Market** (more buyers than sellers right now) and **Durability** (can it become a real business that lasts and grows). He frames the list as subjective ("based on my experience and what I've seen") and says every model can work; the question is which problems you want. The axes are pulled out as a reusable lens in [[wiki/concepts/ai-business-ranking-criteria|AI business ranking criteria]]. **[00:28-01:02]**

## The full ranking

Scores are T / M / D (Technical / Market / Durability), as stated by Nate. Two entries (#12 and #3) have scores he did not state cleanly in the captions; the gap in #12 is noted in its row.

Order check: the video presents exactly these 13, from #13 down to #1 with no gaps.

## The thesis (last third)

- **The bottom sells the thing you build; the top sells your judgment.** **[13:05-13:14]** Developed in [[wiki/concepts/sell-judgment-not-the-build|sell judgment, not the build]].
- **Path for a beginner:** start with websites for momentum, use those same clients to climb to workshops and consulting, "maybe even eventually" a chief AI officer seat. **[13:14-13:25]** See [[wiki/techniques/start-low-climb-to-judgment-businesses|start low, climb to judgment businesses]].
- **Focus beats model choice:** every model can work. The successful entrepreneurs he knows won because they had more focus than competitors and stuck with it, not because they picked the best business. "Pick one and make it work." **[13:25-13:50]** The same conclusion as [[wiki/techniques/narrow-the-niche-before-general|narrow the niche before going general]], applied to the business model instead of the product niche.
- **Closing pitch:** the window closes as more people learn this; a free community and a paid "Plus" community are plugged. **[13:55-14:30]**

## Skeptic note

Nate sells a free community and a paid "Plus" community whose pitch depends on this list (he plugs both at 14:04-14:27, and a free "first AI automation client" SOP mid-video at 07:05). He ranks his own business (#3) with a disclosure but still scores it a 5 on durability. His top picks (consulting, CAIO) are the models that need credibility he already has, which makes his advice to a beginner odd: start with websites, the model he scores lowest on durability. The list is survivorship-flavoured: no churn, failure rate or earnings distribution is shown for any model, and "the founders I have talked to succeeded through focus" cannot be falsified. The per-model numbers are headlines and price ranges, not outcomes. Use the three axes and the build-vs-judgment lens; treat the ordering as one opinion, not a market study.
