# Source: "The FASTEST Way to Go Viral on TikTok in 2026 (TikTok Algorithm Explained)" by Robert Benjamin

An 11.7-minute YouTube video, published 2026-09-14. Claims below are timestamped against it.

**Source grading.** A creator-empirical source with a sales layer. The speaker says he has "gone viral hundreds of times" (not verifiable), and roughly two of the 11 minutes (04:07-07:08) are a pitch for ContentBuddy, a paid trend-research tool with a 7-day free trial behind a pinned-comment link. Treat anything that sells the tool as marketing. The "algorithm" is reverse-engineered from his own posts and a few hand-picked comparables; he cites no TikTok document. Official TikTok statements were checked against the 2020 newsroom post ["How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou"](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you). That post is old and deliberately vague (it publishes no weights), so "official" below means "TikTok has said this much, no more".

**Official, in TikTok's own words** (the 2020 newsroom post, still the main public statement): inputs are user interactions (likes, shares, comments, follows, content created), video information (captions, sounds, hashtags) and device and account settings. Follower count and past hits are not direct factors. Strong indicators such as finishing a longer video weigh more than weak ones. Duplicated and spam content is not recommended. Anything past that is outside the public record. Later TikTok pages also say unoriginal, reused content is not eligible for the feed (a policy dated 2025-08-14, cited secondhand via a third-party article and not checked at source).

## Reading it next to the YouTube pages

- **Same:** retention and completion are the engine ([[wiki/concepts/shorts-retention-vvsa-avd]] on YouTube, finish-the-video weighting on TikTok). Both sources say a new account can break out if the content works, and both attach a "bot suspicion" story to new or odd accounts ([[wiki/concepts/zero-view-jail]] against the shadowban lore). The account-level story is largely folklore on both.
- **Different:** TikTok treats text metadata (captions, hashtags, sounds, name, bio) as an active ranking and search input; the dmscales Shorts model has none of that and is all about swipe-away and AVD. Benjamin also has no equivalent of a seed audience or view-jail band; his "viral" means anecdotal views on single posts, not a staged test.
- **Conflicts:** none with dmscales on retention. "Match the comparables' length" is softer than dmscales' "100% AVD on sub-30-second shorts", a stricter retention target; they agree only that short beats long unless the content holds.
