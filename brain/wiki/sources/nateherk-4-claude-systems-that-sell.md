# Nate Herk: "4 Claude Systems That Sell For $3000+ Right Now (With PROOF)"

YouTube, 11:00, Nate Herk | AI Automation, published 2026-10-27: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQJRLZ2q_Ns. Four productized Claude builds, each "built and sold by members of my own community" [00:06-00:11], with what it does, who pays and a pitch angle per system. Sponsor-adjacent: the video promotes his paid community, a free "first client" SOP and his book [04:10, 08:07, 10:41].

## The four systems

| # | System | Buyer | Stated price | Who (per Nate) | Proof grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[wiki/concepts/voice-note-to-document-system\|Voice note to document]] [00:24-02:11] | Construction (trades) company | £4,500 (about $6,000) | "Jodie" | Creator-claimed. First name only, no invoice or client in the transcript |
| 2 | [[wiki/concepts/intake-and-follow-up-funnel-system\|Intake and follow-up funnel]] [02:11-04:41] | Semi-retired doctor | $2,200, then a repeat job of $6,000 | "Luis"/"Lewis" (spelled both ways), found on Upwork | Creator-claimed. The only one with a repeat-purchase claim and a named sourcing channel |
| 3 | [[wiki/concepts/creator-os-system\|Creator OS]] [04:41-06:32] | Coach teaching women in their 50s affiliate marketing | About $3,600 + $197/mo retainer | "Charles" | Creator-claimed. Scope is bundled (site, store, 101-page guide, emails), so it is not a clean Claude-system price |
| 4 | [[wiki/concepts/company-brain-system\|Company brain]] [06:34-09:11] | Any team business | $15,000 (17 sold, 9 on monthly plans); $2,000 custom | "Janette"; "Kieran" | Creator-claimed. Strongest numbers, least checkable; one figure is garbled in the captions (see skeptic note) |

The headline "around $3,700" [00:01] and the title's "$3000+" are not an average of these four: the figures run from $2,000 to $15,000. Treat it as a hook.

## Key takeaways

- **[00:08-00:11]** Sourcing: all four come from Nate's own community members, so selection bias is built in (no failures shown) and he profits when viewers join.
- **[01:05-01:35, 03:00-03:15, 05:56-06:10, 07:17-07:30]** Every pitch makes the same move: translate the system into a number the owner already feels (25 paid foreman hours a week; 40 paying leads lost out of 40 form fills with 10 paying; 19 extra guide sales cover the fee; 40 payroll hours a week lost to searching). See [[wiki/techniques/pitch-ai-systems-on-business-outcome|pitch AI systems on business outcome]].
- **[01:41-01:55]** The price covers hidden costs too (time, risk, change management), not just the fee. It comes back in the company-brain pitch [07:53-08:05].
- **[01:52-02:10, 08:14-08:34]** Scope rule for selling: automate the one document or question set the buyer hates most, then add the next. Build once and reuse per client; build one for yourself first as a live demo.
- **[04:00-04:08]** Lead sourcing for system 2 was Upwork, with a short recorded demo as the outreach asset. **[06:12-06:30]** For system 3 the outreach asset is three sample pieces written in the target's voice from 20 to 30 of their posts, then a one-month single-platform trial.
- **[08:36-09:09]** The company brain has two pricing modes: a template at $15,000 (Janette: 17 sold, add-ons like per-role views and per-lead research briefs, 9 of 17 retained monthly) or custom at $2,000 (Kieran). Nate: "high ticket with a template" or "lower ticket and custom"; the relationship grows from there.
- **[09:16-09:33]** Niching: don't force it early; niche down once you have data and experience. Echoes [[wiki/techniques/narrow-the-niche-before-general|narrow the niche before going general]].
- **[09:34-09:46]** Lead with the business outcome: "think of yourself as a business growth person who happens to use AI when AI is the right fit."
- **[09:48-10:08]** These four are "a great foot in the door" and portfolio pieces, but the most trust comes from attacking a business's constraint.
- **[10:09-10:37]** The constraint test: [[wiki/concepts/demand-vs-supply-constrained-business|demand-constrained vs supply-constrained]]. The content system and the intake funnel only help demand-constrained firms; supply-constrained ones need the voice-note system or the company brain.

## Skeptic note

- **"With PROOF" is not proof.** The transcript carries only audio; no invoice, contract, payment or client is visible in it, and no on-screen evidence could be verified. Every price is a creator-relayed claim about a community member, first names only. Grade for all four: creator-claimed, unverified.
- **Survivorship plus a funnel.** Four winners hand-picked from his own paid community, which the video sells. No count of members who tried and sold nothing.
- **Garbled figure.** "He's made around $137 since June and he just got hired to build 30 of these for one company" [08:57-09:03] cannot be literal at a $2,000 price. Probably "$137k" or similar, mis-captioned. Unresolved; do not cite.
- **Stacked scope.** System 3's $3,600 includes a 6-page site, a print-on-demand store, a 101-page guide, a nine-email sequence, SEO for 12 pages and a lead quiz [05:11-05:25]. The Claude system is a fraction of it.
- **Retention numbers exist only for system 4** (9 of 17 monthly), plus Luis's rehire. Systems 1 and 3 have no outcome data, only the sale.
- **Unsourced statistic.** "About 50% of employees' time is the scavenger hunt" [07:32-07:39] is backed only by "I actually saw this study".
- **Pitch math is the buyer's problem, not evidence.** The 25 hours, 40 hours and 19 sales are hypotheticals Nate shows you how to compute for a prospect, not measured results of these jobs. One real result is claimed: Luis's client saved "about 15 hours a week" [02:55].
- **Selling AI to non-technical owners works on trust**, and "a builder fits any buyer" is the unproven part. Treat this as a menu of shapes to adapt, not a validated market.
