# Source: "I Studied 10,000 AI Websites… Here's what I learned" (Nate Herk)

YouTube video (11.3 min) by Nate Herk | AI Automation, published 2026-11-09: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mImbbszCjCI. A sequel in spirit to [[wiki/sources/nateherk-fable-5-1-kills-website-slop|"Fable 5.1 kills website slop"]]: that one was about prompting and tooling, this one is about what a site must contain to convert.

Premise: five patterns that every AI-built site that "drives a real result" follows, so it can be sold to a business for about $1,000.

## The five patterns

- **[00:25-02:33] Mirror test:** the first-screen copy answers who it is for, what they get and what to do next. Case studies: Truckers Report +79.3%, City Click +90%, Campaign Monitor +31.4%. See [[wiki/concepts/mirror-test-first-screen|mirror test]].
- **[02:36-04:18] Highlighter rule:** one accent colour, used only on the click target; a 60-30-10 split. See [[wiki/concepts/highlighter-rule-60-30-10|highlighter rule]] and [[wiki/techniques/apply-60-30-10-palette-with-claude|the palette how-to]].
- **[04:18-06:46] Front, middle, back:** three depth layers, one thing breaks the edge, shadow only on what matters. See [[wiki/techniques/front-middle-back-layers|front, middle, back]] and [[wiki/concepts/layering-for-premium-feel|layering for premium feel]].
- **[06:46-09:13] Motion with a job:** one job per animation, one thing moves per screen, words readable instantly, and a reduced-motion off switch. See [[wiki/concepts/motion-with-a-job|motion with a job]].
- **[09:14-10:58] Free sample:** a quiz, calculator or tool before the contact form, justified by the IKEA effect (a Doc Braces calculator at about 45% conversion, per Outgrow). See [[wiki/concepts/free-sample-pattern|free sample pattern]].
- **[10:58-11:17] Close:** "use AI for most of the work, you just have to know how to review it."

## Connections

Extends [[wiki/concepts/three-ps-pain-person-promise|the three P's]] (pattern 1 is the three P's applied to the first screen), [[wiki/concepts/ai-website-slop|AI website slop]] and [[wiki/concepts/scroll-feedback|scroll feedback]]. Most general design-quality checklists already cover hierarchy, depth and motion that clarifies; the first-screen copy test, the single-accent rule, one job per animation and the free-sample tool are the parts they usually miss.

## Skeptic note

- "10,000 websites" is a hook, not a method: no dataset, selection or counts are shown. The five patterns are Nate's synthesis plus borrowed case studies. The sample-size claim is unverifiable.
- All numbers (79.3%, 90%, 31.4%, 45%) are secondhand and unlinked in the captions, and the 45% comes from a vendor (Outgrow) that sells the calculator. Treat them as illustrative.
- Study references (Nielsen Norman, Purdue parallax, the IKEA effect, an inner-ear survey) are named but not cited; "about a third of adults over 40" is the creator's paraphrase.
- Commercial angle: he promotes a free [[wiki/tools/scroll-craft|ScrollCraft]] skill (a lead magnet to his community) and sells the "$1,000 websites" dream to his audience. Nothing is sponsored by name.
- Shown on screen: the AI Automation Society site and Apple's AirPods Pro page. Everything else is described.
