# TikTok bot suspicion and the "shadowban" story

**Claim (folklore):** TikTok wants to see you engage like a human. An account that only posts, or that engages on a fixed schedule, looks like a bot and gets silently suppressed, and the suppression does not show in any check. The fix: for 1-2 weeks, comment on, watch, like and reply to other people's content, 3-5 days a week, at irregular times.

Source: [[wiki/sources/robertbenjamin-tiktok-algorithm-2026|Robert Benjamin]] (02:40-04:05).

## Grade

- **Not official.** TikTok's published description of the For You feed lists interactions by the viewer as signals for what that viewer sees. It does not say the creator's own engagement lifts their reach, and it describes no bot-suspicion penalty for fresh accounts.
- **Unfalsifiable as stated.** A shadowban that cannot be detected cannot be disproven, so the claim cannot teach anything. Recorded, not adopted as a mechanism.
- **Cheap to try.** Engaging in your niche also builds the account's topic history in practice (what it likes and watches shapes what it is served), and it costs a few minutes. Reasonable hygiene, not a proven fix.

## Parallel on YouTube

The same shape appears in [[wiki/concepts/zero-view-jail|zero view jail]] (a new account fails an identity screen and gets zero distribution) and in the [[wiki/concepts/youtube-trust-score|trust score]] model, graded as folklore with a kernel. Both platforms grow an "invisible account-level penalty" story, and neither has published evidence. The kernel on each: platforms do run spam and bot detection, and a brand-new account with no history is a harder case for them.
