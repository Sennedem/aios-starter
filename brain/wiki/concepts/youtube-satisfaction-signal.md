# The satisfaction signal (the metric above the metrics)

vidIQ's claim: viewer satisfaction sits above CTR and watch time. YouTube runs surveys on videos people recently watched, and the answers feed the recommender. "It's measuring whether they're glad they watched" (8:49-9:08).

Source: [[wiki/sources/vidiq-youtube-algorithm-guide|vidIQ guide]].

## Grade: official, with an overreach

- **Official:** YouTube's help page says its satisfaction surveys "help the system understand satisfaction, not just watch time"; its product page describes "valued watch time" from 1-5 star surveys. A YouTube product manager (Rachel Alves) is reported as saying "we no longer consider all Watch Time on YouTube to be equal" and that surveys are among the most important training data. Checked by web search: the help page directly, the quotes through secondary write-ups.
- **Overreach:** "more than all of them combined" (8:51) is vidIQ's rhetoric; YouTube publishes no weights.

## The source's mechanics (creator-inferred)

- **Padding backfires.** Tom Scott's garlic-bread video keeps its promise in the first 5 seconds; a padded 20-minute version might win watch time and lose the survey. High watch time with low satisfaction "can get suppressed over time" (9:44-10:47).
- **The answer is not simply "make it shorter".** Every minute should be worth the viewer's time.
- **Good abandonment:** leaving a how-to at 3 minutes because you want to go and try the thing is not necessarily a failure; "the algorithm is getting smarter about context" (10:51-11:09). Unverified: no official statement found that retention is context-adjusted this way.
- **Expected value over time (16:26-17:00):** the stated objective is whether the viewer comes back tomorrow, so a short satisfied session beats two drained hours. This matches the recommender paper's framing of long-term value; the "come back tomorrow" wording is vidIQ's.

## Tension with the Shorts model

The dmscales model ([[wiki/concepts/shorts-retention-vvsa-avd|VVSA and AVD]]) makes near-full watch-through the target for a sub-30-second short. The satisfaction frame says long-form should not be padded for watch time. These are different surfaces, not a contradiction: one rewards completing a tiny unit, the other punishes stretching a big one.
