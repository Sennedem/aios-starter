# Motion with a job

Scroll animation is the fastest way to make a site feel premium, and also the fastest way to make it look AI-generated. The rule: every animation earns its place by doing exactly **one** job. It shows the product, points the eye to the next step, or shows that something just happened. An animation with zero jobs, or five, gets cut. From [[wiki/sources/nateherk-10000-ai-websites|Nate Herk, I Studied 10,000 AI Websites]] (pattern 4, [06:46-09:13]).

## The three rules

1. **One thing moves per screen.** Pick the product, the result or the before-and-after. Everything else stays still.
2. **Never make them wait for the words.** Headline, text and button are readable the instant the screen loads. Fly-ins and 3D scenes belong further down the page, never on the first screen.
3. **Give an off switch.** Respect the operating system's reduced-motion setting, and optionally add a visible "motion off" toggle next to light and dark mode.

## Evidence

- Positive example shown on screen: Apple's AirPods Pro page, where only the product rotates as you scroll.
- Self-admitted miss: he cut a 3D spinning ring from his own site because it was "just a lot."
- Nielsen Norman Group (creator-cited): moving things pull attention, often away from what the visitor came to do.
- Purdue parallax study (creator-cited): users rated parallax more fun, but not easier to use or better looking.
- Inner-ear figure (creator-cited, "one big US health survey", unlinked): about a third of adults over 40 show signs of balance problems, which is why reduced-motion settings exist.
