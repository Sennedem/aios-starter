# Format saturation: when a copied packaging stops working

If your format (thumbnail style, title structure, angle) has been copied by thousands of channels, the algorithm filling one viewer's feed shows one of them, and defaults to "the channel with the larger data set". The source's example is the "I survived 100 days in Minecraft" template (a weak character on day 1, a glowing one on day 100): popularised by one creator, copied by thousands of small channels, now dominated by a 6M-subscriber channel. Search the phrase and the small clones are "nowhere to be found" (11:13-12:39).

Source: [[wiki/sources/vidiq-youtube-algorithm-guide|vidIQ guide]].

## The advice (12:41-13:10)

If you look identical to a channel ten times your size, the big one gets shown instead. Big channels are locked into their format by audience expectations; you are not. Find the angles they skip, use a thumbnail style that stands out against the clones, and frame for curiosity rather than format fatigue.

The source also makes the reverse point (2:05-3:07, with Organic Chemistry Tutor and Daily Dose of Internet as examples): plain, descriptive titles and one consistent channel message help, because clarity beats cleverness when the algorithm has to guess who a video is for.

## Grade

Creator-inferred. The "one similar video per feed" mechanism is plausible (feed diversification is a known recommender practice) but not documented by YouTube, and the Minecraft case is an anecdote with survivorship bias. The "defaults to the larger channel" claim matches the dmscales point that oversaturated niches favour the most-trusted channels ([[wiki/concepts/youtube-trust-score|trust score]]): two creator sources agree, neither is official.
