# TikTok length and format matching

**Claim (creator-empirical):** for a given topic, viral videos cluster around a format (comedy skit, tutorial), and each format has a length the audience will finish. Make your video the same topic, format and length as the winners, or you lose the completion that TikTok ranks on.

Source: [[wiki/sources/robertbenjamin-tiktok-algorithm-2026|Robert Benjamin]] (00:45-02:38).

## His method

1. Search the topic in TikTok and look at the videos with the most views.
2. Name the format of each (skit or tutorial) and note its length.
3. Pick your format, then match that format's winning length (his example: 20 seconds for comedy skits, about 3 minutes for tutorials).

## What holds and what does not

- **Holds (official in direction):** TikTok says finishing a longer video is weighted more than weak signals. Completion rate is the thing, the same engine as [[wiki/concepts/shorts-retention-vvsa-avd|YouTube Shorts retention]].
- **Does not hold as shown:** his "proof" compares different creators at different lengths (a 45-second tutorial at 14k against a 3-minute tutorial at 200k) and calls length the cause. Hooks, accounts and timing differ, so the data cannot isolate length. Treat "a 3-4 second difference is hundreds vs hundreds of thousands" as hyperbole.
- **Practical reading:** length is a proxy for whether you can hold the viewer to the end. The honest rule is to stop before your content stops earning attention, and to use comparables as a sanity check on format, not as a target in seconds.

## Link to other pages

This points the same way as the dmscales rule that short Shorts should hit near-100% AVD ([[wiki/concepts/shorts-retention-vvsa-avd]]).
