# 30k view jail

The pattern where short after short lands at roughly the same ceiling: a slow start, a spike, then a flat line somewhere near 30,000 views. Creators widely read it as YouTube suppressing them. The source argues that reading is backwards.

Source: [[wiki/sources/dmscales-shorts-algorithm|dmscales, "The Entire Shorts Algorithm Explained in 180 Seconds"]]. Creator vocabulary, not a YouTube term, and the 30,000 figure has no official basis (see [[wiki/concepts/youtube-trust-score|the grading]]).

## The reframe

Reaching about 30k means the short passed every [[wiki/concepts/seed-audience-testing|seed audience test]] it was given. A short that did badly would not get there. Being stuck at 30k therefore means the content is good enough to go viral and YouTube does not yet know who to show it to. The on-screen counter-claim is explicit: shorts under about 35k views "did really well in the seed audience tests", and treating a run of 30k shorts as proof that "YouTube hates me" is called out as a trap.

## What breaks the ceiling

More data, more than better videos in isolation. Once the seed audiences have identified the right target audience for the channel, the next short that audience responds to gets pushed much wider. This is the mechanical argument for daily uploading: each upload is another data point that narrows the targeting, and only volume produces them.

## Caveat

Tell "capped at 30k because YouTube is still learning" apart from "capped at 10k-20k because the video is weak". They have different causes and different fixes. See [[wiki/techniques/diagnosing-a-flatlined-short|diagnosing a flatlined short]].

Its opposite is [[wiki/concepts/zero-view-jail|zero view jail]], where a channel gets no distribution at all: that is an identity problem, this one is a data problem.
