# Scroll feedback

Visitors need to feel that they control the site as they scroll, so the page has to respond to their input. How strong that response is stays a judgment call, and the maximum is a poor default. From [[wiki/sources/nateherk-fable-5-1-kills-website-slop|Nate Herk, Fable 5.1 kills website slop]].

## The rule

You need "some sort of scroll responsiveness". Without feedback as they scroll, the page feels static and unpolished. Nothing flashy is required:

- Text that fades or slides in as it enters the viewport is enough on its own.
- A subtle, mouse-reactive background can add depth, "but it's not distracting."
- A nav bar that stays locked through the scroll reads as more premium, for reasons Nate admits he can't fully put into words: "it just feels good."

## Restraint

Nate cites viewer pushback on an earlier, more aggressive build: "I hate this 3D world where I scroll and it makes me fly." He keeps scroll motion, and concludes that **the right amount of feedback depends on the business**, tied to [[wiki/concepts/three-ps-pain-person-promise|the three P's]]. What feels premium to one audience reads as gimmicky to another. A B2B SaaS buyer and a consumer lifestyle-brand buyer do not want the same scroll intensity. Motion serves something, at a level calibrated to the audience.
