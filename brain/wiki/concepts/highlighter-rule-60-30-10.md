# Highlighter rule (one accent colour, 60-30-10)

Pick **one** colour that stands out from everything else and use it only on the thing you want clicked (a button, a booking link). AI treats colour as decoration and spreads the accent over headline, icons, borders, background and button, so the button stops standing out ("highlighter happy"). From [[wiki/sources/nateherk-10000-ai-websites|Nate Herk, I Studied 10,000 AI Websites]] (pattern 2, [02:36-04:18]).

## The 60-30-10 split (borrowed from interior design)

| Share | Role on a website |
|---|---|
| 60% | background, main colour |
| 30% | structure: text, menu, cards, section blocks |
| 10% | accent: buttons, links, a few specific words |

## Worked example Nate gives

The first version of his AI Automation Society site had blue everywhere and "looked so cheap". He went back to mostly black and white with blue only on the small things people should notice, and it felt "way more premium and more clear." This rests on his own before-and-after description; no side-by-side is shown in the captions. A creator-claimed taste judgement.

How to apply it: [[wiki/techniques/apply-60-30-10-palette-with-claude|Apply a 60-30-10 palette with Claude]].

Note: the rule fixes one *role* for the accent (the click target), not a fixed amount of colour. A brand can still use its colour big in the 60 or 30 slot.
