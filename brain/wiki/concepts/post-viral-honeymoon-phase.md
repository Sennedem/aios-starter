# Post-viral honeymoon phase

After a channel's first viral video, YouTube becomes more and more willing to push later uploads wide, because the channel has proven once that it can be trusted with an audience. Temporary re-jailing still happens, but recovery is much faster: keep posting videos as strong as the outlier and the next breakout comes sooner.

Source: [[wiki/sources/dmscales-shorts-algorithm|dmscales, "The Entire Shorts Algorithm Explained in 180 Seconds"]] (02:07-02:38). Creator model, not YouTube documentation. The creator's own numbers for the week after a breakout (25K, 524K, 633K, 467K, 263K, 3,521K and 12K views on consecutive daily uploads) show both the lift and its volatility.

## The end state

Once [[wiki/concepts/youtube-trust-score|trust]] is high enough, view jailing reportedly stops entirely: every upload gets wide distribution, and monetization (a 10M-view threshold is referenced on screen) becomes a matter of upload count.

## The trap

The source frames this phase as the most dangerous moment to relax:

- Posting less in a high-trust state can push you back into view jail.
- Consistently dropping weak videos does the same.
- The failure mode is distribution collapsing to subscribers only, visible as a sudden ceiling and a subscriber-heavy traffic mix.
- A severe enough regression can mean effectively restarting.

On this model trust is asymmetric: it builds slowly through many clean uploads and drains quickly through a run of bad ones. So the right response to a first viral video is to raise the quality bar and keep the effort up.

Whether a pause really hurts is disputed. For Instagram, [[wiki/sources/buildyourtribe-instagram-algorithm-2026|Build Your Tribe]] says a two-week break is not punished. Neither claim is official.
