# YouTube traffic sources: browse, suggested, search

The Traffic sources report in YouTube Studio Analytics splits views into three buckets. Each says something different about how YouTube sees the channel and rewards different behaviour.

Source: [[wiki/sources/vidiq-youtube-algorithm-guide|vidIQ guide]] (5:36-8:30).

| Source | What it is | The source's claim | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Browse** | The home page, before any search | Not only for big channels: small channels get "early discovery tests", a video seeded to a small group whose watch history suggests they would enjoy it (6:16-6:44) | Corroborated in direction (Beaupre: recommendations are per viewer, built mostly on watch history). The seeding story echoes [[wiki/concepts/seed-audience-testing\|seed audience testing]] but gives no 30k figure |
| **Browse is not driven by subscribers** | | "YouTube has shifted to a viewer history model": someone who watched your last two videos gets the next one on their feed whether or not they subscribed, and a million inactive subscribers earn little browse traffic (6:44-7:11) | Corroborated: Beaupre has said watch history is the strongest signal and that subscribers click only a minority of uploads. "Shifted" as a dated change is vidIQ's framing |
| **Suggested** | Beside or below another video | YouTube moved from topic matching to session matching ("if they watch A, would they like B?"), clustered by micro-niche (topic, tone and audience), so a relaxing architecture channel can sit beside productivity channels (7:14-8:26) | Creator-inferred. The Google recommender paper (co-visitation) supports the principle; the "micro-niche" wording is vidIQ's |
| **Search** | People typing the problem you solve | Rewards clarity and consistent language; slower but durable, "can drive views for years" (8:28-8:47) | Plausible and widely repeated; no number given. vidIQ sells the tool that finds keywords |

## Use

Screenshot the traffic-source breakdown and ask whether what you make matches where viewers come from (13:12-13:36). Browse-heavy with irregular posting burns the best placement; search-heavy with no subscriber conversion "builds someone else's library". The weekly version of this check is in [[wiki/techniques/youtube-baseline-outlier-spreadsheet|the baseline spreadsheet]].
