# AI business ranking criteria: Technical, Market, Durability

The three axes Nate Herk scores every business model on in [[wiki/sources/nateherk-ai-businesses-ranked|his ranking video]], each from 1 to 5. Useful as a reusable checklist for any offer, independent of his actual scores. **[00:41-00:55]**

| Axis | Question it answers | Scale quirk |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | How much skill do you need to deliver something people will pay for? | Inverted: a *lower* score means *more* skill required |
| **Market** | Are there more buyers than sellers right now? | Higher means more buyers per seller |
| **Durability** | Can it become a real business that lasts and keeps growing? | Platform risk, commoditization and churn pull it down; stickiness and trust pull it up |

## Durability signals he uses

- **Down:** a platform can change rules overnight (faceless channels); one platform update kills a thin wrapper (SaaS); incumbents bundle the feature into software the client already pays for (receptionists); building gets easier so clients hire in-house (automation). **[01:51-06:48]**
- **Up:** a clear KPI (lead gen: "more leads equals more customers"); a retainer that is painful to cancel; judgment AI cannot do (change management inside a company); a personal brand that survives tool changes. **[08:38-12:58]**

## The extra rule: one business-outcome metric per offer

Under the AI-OS entry he adds a requirement that sits outside the three axes: "every offer that you have needs one clear metric that you're moving, and this needs to be a business outcome metric, something that they care about." An offer with no KPI is hard to renew, hard to turn into a case study and hard to get referred. **[04:19-04:43]**

## Limits

The scores are one creator's opinion, not data (see the skeptic note on the source page). The axes also mix one-person fit (Technical) with market structure (Market, Durability), so they do not replace the sequential filters in [[wiki/concepts/three-filters-one-person-business|Three filters for a one-person business]]. Those answer "can one person run it"; these answer "is it a good business to be in".
