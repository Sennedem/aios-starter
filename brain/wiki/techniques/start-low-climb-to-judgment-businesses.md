# Start low, climb to judgment businesses

Nate Herk's recommended path for someone new, from [[wiki/sources/nateherk-ai-businesses-ranked|Every AI Business Ranked]]. **[13:14-13:50]**

1. **Start with a low-skill, visible offer.** AI websites rate as the best beginner entry: you can demo one in about 20 minutes before you pitch. The purpose is momentum and first clients, not the long-term business. **[03:20-03:59]**
2. **Use the same clients to move up** to foot-in-the-door offers that expose problems and build trust: workshops (1K for a half day to 5K for a full day), or an AI operating system setup. Workshop clients "almost always" give a bigger project afterwards. **[04:09-04:14, 08:01-08:12]**
3. **Then consulting and audits** (1K-15K). You are paid for diagnosis, and it pays faster than building an audience. **[11:41-11:57]**
4. **Eventually** a fractional or in-house chief AI officer seat. **[12:01-12:26]**
5. **Run a personal brand alongside, not first.** "Proof comes first and trust comes next"; he did agency work, consulting and workshops for years before the brand, and the brand then powers spin-offs. **[10:30-10:52]**
6. **Pick one and stay on it** instead of switching niche or model every few weeks. **[13:25-13:50]**

## Rule of thumb

Each rung should hand you the next client and the next piece of proof. If a rung does not produce a case study or a referral, it is a dead end. His example is an AI operating system sold to an owner who does not believe in AI yet. **[04:28-04:43]**

## Caveat

This is one path from one creator who sells community access, and no conversion rates between rungs are given. Treat it as a sequencing hypothesis. Compare [[wiki/techniques/narrow-the-niche-before-general|Narrowing the niche before going general]] for the same start-narrow logic at product level, and [[wiki/concepts/sell-judgment-not-the-build|Sell judgment, not the build]] for why the higher rungs pay more.
