# Front, middle, back (three-layer page build)

Cheap-looking AI sites put every box, image and button on one flat level, so the eye cannot decide what to look at first. The stage metaphor: painted backdrop (back), furniture (middle), actor (front). From [[wiki/sources/nateherk-10000-ai-websites|Nate Herk, I Studied 10,000 AI Websites]] (pattern 3, [04:18-06:46]). Concept background: [[wiki/concepts/layering-for-premium-feel|Layering for premium feel]].

## Layers

- **Back:** a photo, soft shape or block of colour that sets the mood.
- **Middle:** headline, text, cards.
- **Front:** the one action, normally the call-to-action button.

## Steps

1. Put something real in the back: swap the plain white box for a photo of the work or product, or at least a soft shape.
2. Let one thing break the edge: a card or image hangs halfway into the next section, so the page stops being a stack of boxes.
3. Lift only what matters: give the main button or main card a soft shadow so it sits in front.

## Worked example (hairstylist booking site, Nate's hypothetical)

Back: a photo of the stylist's chair and her work. Middle: the headline "Fill your calendar without answering DMs" overlapping the photo edge, and a booking card with the next open slots hanging into the next section. Front: one orange "Book your spot" button with a soft shadow, with orange used nowhere else.

## Demonstrated on screen

The AI Automation Society homepage: mountains in the back, timeline and laptop on separate layers, text on its own layer. A front tree line once covered the laptop layer; it was fixed by cutting a V into the trees so the button stays front and centre.

Nate also mentions a free website-design skill, [[wiki/tools/scroll-craft|ScrollCraft]] (taste, typography, spacing, depth, scroll animation). Its contents were not shown in this video.
