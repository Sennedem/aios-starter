# Narrowing the niche before going general

Pick a narrow, specific niche and get good at it before expanding. In [[wiki/sources/nateherk-one-person-business-claude|Nate Herk's one-person business video]] this is the stated reason the product tests **customer-support agents** specifically, instead of "AI agents" in general. **[10:00-10:53]**

## The reasoning, as given

"Saying all agents is very broad. You know, sales agent, finance agent, sport agent, they all have different types of tests, different processes. And if we tried to cover everything, the product would become generic and the promise would get a little bit more vague." **[10:07-10:20]** He gives three reasons to narrow:

1. **Broad dilutes the promise.** A generic "we eval AI agents" claim is vague because different agent types (sales, finance, support) need different tests. Covering all of them at once produces a shallow, generic product.
2. **The broad category already has better-resourced competitors.** "There are already other products out there that do evals or QAs for AI agents. And those other companies probably already have customers, more capital, and a reputation." **[10:24-10:34]** Owning a corner they have not specialised in is a better position than competing head-on.
3. **A narrow category is repeatable and lets you get genuinely good at it.** Customer-support agents give "a repeatable, high-risk situation that we can test... refunds, billing, disputes, account deletion, private data requests, knowing when to involve a human": a fixed, learnable list of risk scenarios instead of an open-ended one. **[10:34-10:53]**

## The sequencing

This is framed as a starting position, not a permanent ceiling: "we can then, if we need to, later expand into other agents. But we need to get a good foundation laid." **[10:48-10:53]** Prove the model on one specific, painful, well-understood risk category, then expand.

[[wiki/sources/nateherk-ai-businesses-ranked|Every AI Business Ranked]] makes the same point one level up: "pick one and focus on it like crazy instead of jumping to a new niche or business model every few weeks" is his explanation for why founders succeed, more than the choice of business. See [[wiki/techniques/start-low-climb-to-judgment-businesses|Start low, climb to judgment businesses]].

## How this connects to the three P's

This technique makes the "person" and "promise" half of [[wiki/concepts/three-ps-pain-person-promise|pain, person, promise]] concrete. The person narrows from "anyone deploying AI agents" to "an AI automation agency deploying customer-support agents for clients". The promise narrows from a generic "we test your agents" to a named list of risk scenarios. A vague person and a vague promise are the failure this technique fixes, seen from the positioning side.
