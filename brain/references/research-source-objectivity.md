# Research source objectivity

When you research any entity (a company, a product, a fund, a claim), never build the picture mostly from the subject's own sources. Self-published material is structurally biased: a firm will never publish that it greenwashes or misreports its sustainability numbers, because that would hurt its reputation and its sales. Triangulate every material claim against independent outside sources.

## Why it matters

A report that leans on the subject's own website and materials is uncritical. In school work it loses marks; in business work it loses credibility. A typical first pass on a large company comes back with four of six sources from the company itself and two from sites close to it. That is the pattern to catch before anything is written on top of it.

## How to apply

- **Triangulate** every material claim against independent sources. Aim for a balanced base, not one where most sources belong to the subject.
- **Vet each outside source for its own bias.** Discount competitors and parties with a conflict or a grudge: former employees who left in protest, politically motivated actors, advocacy groups with an agenda.
- **Prefer objective, trustworthy sources:** peer-reviewed journals, reputable wire and financial journalism (AP, Reuters, FT), independent research institutes.
- A biased source can still be cited when it is useful, but **state the bias explicitly** ("advocacy group", "politically motivated", "former employee in a dispute"). Never present a biased source as neutral.

This applies to every research run, and to any research that feeds school or business work. Related: [[wiki/concepts/seen-inferred-unknown]] for labelling what a source shows against what you inferred.

## In this system

Any research leg Claude dispatches, and the fact-checker seat in `/jury`, should be held to this rule: a claim backed only by the subject's own pages is reported as unverified.
