# Getting and keeping customers: the evidence

The numbers behind the `/sales-engine` playbooks. The skill's own reference files hold the operating rules (the channel order, the plays by business type, Claude's jobs, the retention tactics); this page holds the benchmarks and sources those rules rest on, so a plan can quote a number with its grade. From a research synthesis of five parallel legs and 100+ sources, November 2026.

**Evidence grades:** strong = peer-reviewed or a large independent dataset; moderate = consistent platform or vendor data from several sources; weak = one vendor or agency; anecdotal = a single self-reported case. Most sales numbers are vendor data (Gong, Belkins, Cognism, Instantly, ManyChat): read them as ceilings, not forecasts.

## The answer

**There is no easy channel.** The easy customers are the ones who already trust you or are already looking, and the cheapest win is to stop losing the ones you already reach. Retention is decided in the first 7 to 60 days, by visible progress plus a fixed slot or schedule, more than by price or content volume.

## Phone to businesses

- **Dials per meeting (vendor, US and UK):** Belkins 2026 (175k dials): 9.9% of dials connect, 58% of connects become conversations, 4.6% of conversations book a meeting, so about 370 dials per meeting. Gong: about 400 dials per meeting on average, about 45 for the top quartile.
- **Attempts:** 93% of conversations happen by the third attempt (Cognism), so cap it at 3 to 5 attempts per number.
- **Timing:** Tuesday to Thursday, 10:00 to 11:00 and 14:00 to 16:00 local time.
- **On the call (Gong, 300M calls, vendor):** stating the reason for the call doubles success (2.1x). A permission opener ("can I take 20 seconds to explain why I'm calling?") or a context opener far outperforms "is this a good time?", which is the worst opener measured (2.15% success). Sell the next meeting, not the discovery. On "not interested, just send me an e-mail": agree, ask one either-or question, and ask to call back.
- **A foreign number reads as a scam** to many buyers (inference). Call from a local number in the buyer's country.
- **The list is usually the real constraint, not the dialling.** Filtering a region for the business types you may legally call can leave a tenth of the list. Widen the region or segment before planning a daily dial target.
- **Pay-after or first clients free:** no rigorous evidence either way, only blog anecdotes. Write the success criteria and the later price down before the session, cap the free ones at about three, and close by booking the next paid session and collecting a named quote plus one outcome number.

## Content-led funnels and communities

**Do the funnel math before betting on reach.** Multiply the stage rates: views, then follows per 1,000 views, then free sign-ups, then paid (2 to 5% of free members convert on community platforms; weak). In one worked case, a small account converting 1.6 follows per 1,000 views needed about 520,000 views for 10 paying members (range 105,000 to 2 million), about 15 months at its view rate. Volume is rarely the lever; conversion per stage is.

**Lifetime value caps paid acquisition.** LTV = price × median customer lifetime. For a paid community where the median leaver stays about 50 days (one platform's analytics, n=1,365 departures, moderate), a $29 monthly price gives about $50 per member. Ads at about $5 per free sign-up and 3% free-to-paid conversion cost about $170 per paying member. That case is a clear "no ads until retention or conversion doubles".

**Do not gate content behind a follow.** Meta's Spam Community Standard prohibits, verbatim: *"Requiring users to engage (in the form of likes, shares, follows, or any other public-facing form of engagement) to gain access to specific, exclusive content"* (transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam). Several comment-to-DM tools ship a follow gate anyway; one user reported a spam warning within a day (anecdotal). The safer shape: send the link to everyone who asks and ask for the follow in the same message. Meta also limits automated replies to one private reply per comment, within 7 days, and non-followers receive it in their message requests, so the first line must name what they asked for.

**Comment-to-DM benchmarks (vendor best cases):** DM open rate 70 to 90%, link click 15 to 25%. No published numbers for accounts under about 1,000 followers.

**Cohort plus trial:** a dated cohort with a trial lifted free-to-paid conversion from 2% to 6% for one creator (anecdotal).

## Retention benchmarks

| Business | Number | Source and grade |
|---|---|---|
| Lesson and learning centres | Median 3.2% monthly churn; **60% of churn happens in the first 60 days**; 64% annual renewal | Clear Center Services 2025, 559 centres, vendor; moderate |
| Paid communities | 4 to 8% monthly churn is typical; the median leaver stays 50 days and 38% leave by day 30; members active in their first 14 days churn 3.9% vs 7.5% | RetentionCheck 2026 (weak); one platform's analytics, 2026 (moderate, correlation) |
| Language-learning apps | 43% of adult users quit within 3 months | *Studies in Second Language Acquisition* 2024, peer-reviewed; strong |

| Tactic | Effect | Source and grade |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed schedule instead of self-paced | 45% vs 13% completion | Ihantola 2020, online course comparison; strong |
| Annual plan | 1-year retention 33.9% vs 13.8% on monthly plans | RevenueCat, apps; strong for apps, untested elsewhere |
| Regular progress messages to parents | +6.5 points in course pass rate (randomised trial); the retention link is inferred | Kraft & Rogers 2015; moderate |
| Streaks and leaderboards | +3.3% day-14 retention from a streak change; +17% learning time from leaderboards | Duolingo A/B tests; strong for Duolingo |
| Pause instead of cancel | 3 in 4 people who pause come back | Recurly 2026; moderate |
| Win-back with 4 messages matched to the stop reason | 10 to 15% reactivation vs 5 to 8% for a single message | vendor; moderate |
| Week-one onboarding (a personal message plus one action) | Consistent across datasets, observational | moderate |

## Where AI helps and where it backfires

The evidence supports AI as a **research, drafting and triage layer with a human gate**. Fully autonomous outbound is the documented failure: 11x.ai (fake customer logos, 70 to 80% churn; TechCrunch, 2025-03-24), Artisan (LinkedIn restricted its accounts over scraping), the Zurich research bots on Reddit (banned, legal threat), and Apollo and Seamless removed from LinkedIn. Even SaaStr's success story with AI sales agents needs 15 to 20 hours a week of human oversight per person.

Platform rules that bound what an agent may do:
- Google's scaled content abuse policy (mass-generated pages).
- Reddit's spam policy (bots, generative AI posting).
- YouTube's "inauthentic content" rule (from 2025-07-15).
- LinkedIn's User Agreement section 8.2 (automation leads to account restriction).
- EU AI Act article 50: a chatbot must say it is an AI, from 2026-08-02.

**The law moves.** Cold-contact rules differ by country and by buyer type (consumer, sole trader, incorporated company), and they change: at least one EU country tightened business cold calling in 2026. Check the regulator's own pages for the buyer's country before any cold call, e-mail or DM. Replying to someone's own inquiry or call-back request is not unsolicited contact.
