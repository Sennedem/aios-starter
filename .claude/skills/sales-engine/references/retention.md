# Keep mode: retention and expansion

Operating rules for keeping customers.

**The core idea:** retention is mostly decided in the first weeks, and it is driven by visible progress and a fixed slot or schedule more than by price or content volume. Retention has to be earned; a contract is structure, not lock-in.

## The retention audit
1. **Count it.** Per named cohort: who started, who still pays, who left, when, and the stop reason in their own words. At small numbers one leaver moves the rate a lot, so name people, not percentages.
2. **Find the moment.** Where do people leave: week one, after the first goal, at the end of a term or package, around holidays?
3. **Pick the matching tactic below, one at a time,** each with a tripwire line (metric, bar, date).

## Tactics

### Service
- **Intake sets a goal and a date.** Progress is measured against it.
- **A short note after every session** with what was done and what is next. Regular progress messages are linked to better outcomes (for example Kraft and Rogers, 2015, on teacher-to-parent messages); the retention effect is inferred.
- **A fixed weekly slot,** not ad hoc booking.
- **Open the next goal 4 to 6 weeks before the current one ends,** with the session notes as the evidence. Make it a dated check.
- **Re-match before release:** if a pairing or approach stalls, offer an alternative before the customer stops.
- **Log stop reasons** in the customer's notes and read them in a monthly review.

### Community or subscription
- **A personal message within 24 hours of joining, with one action** ("post your goal", "do lesson one").
- **A dated challenge** that starts on a fixed date; members active early churn less.
- **One weekly live or scheduled moment;** a fixed schedule beats self-paced.
- **Streaks or levels** where the platform supports them (effect on the user's platform is untested).
- **An annual option** at launch.
- **Pause instead of cancel,** handled by hand.
- **Win-back at day 7, 30 and 60** after leaving, each message matched to the stop reason.

## Expansion (the cheapest new revenue)
- An upgrade path: a single package to a longer one; free to paid to a premium tier.
- Ask customers who just hit a goal for a referral and a review. Retained customers are the best referrers (referred customers tend to stay longer while their referrer stays; Schmitt, Skiera and Van den Bulte, 2011).

## What Claude does
- Keeps the per-cohort ledger from the user's live customer records.
- Flags the dated moments: a goal ending in 4 to 6 weeks, a member silent for 7 days, a lapsed customer at day 7, 30 or 60.
- Drafts the message through the `writing` skill. The user sends.
