# CLOSER, the MEDDPICC overlay and the objection bank

The call-mode method. CLOSER (from Alex Hormozi) works on its own for consumer sales. MEDDPICC is the optional overlay for B2B deals.

## CLOSER: the call arc

Build an actual script with rough timings, not just the letters.

| Step | What you do | The move |
|---|---|---|
| Clarify | Open on them: why are they here, what are they after? | Get them talking first. Surface the real situation and goal before saying anything about the offer. |
| Label | Reflect their problem back, name the pain | "So you've tried X and it didn't stick because Y." They feel understood, and you have earned the right to prescribe. |
| Overview | Walk through their past attempts and why they fell short | Shows that this time needs to be different, without trashing what they did. Builds the gap. |
| Sell the vacation | Paint the outcome, not the mechanics | Map the solution to their stated goal. They buy the result; keep features to a minimum. |
| Explain away concerns | Treat objections as questions | Validate, reframe, give proof. Do not fight them. (Bank below.) |
| Reinforce | Confirm the decision, set the next steps | Lock the next action (start date, onboarding, payment) and reassure them they chose well. |

Close consultatively: show the gap and offer to help. Keep the call to action soft and low-friction (a reply, a flexible time), never a pushy booking demand. If the user's `learnings.md` says otherwise, follow it.

## MEDDPICC overlay (B2B only)

Map the qualification onto the CLOSER beats. If the project folder holds its own B2B playbook, load it too. Do not apply this to consumer sales; it adds friction with no payoff there.

| Pillar | Surface it during | The question's job |
|---|---|---|
| Metrics | Clarify, Label | Quantify the pain and the payback in money or time. |
| Economic buyer | Clarify | Who signs off? Are we talking to them? Never assume; ask "who decides on something like this?" |
| Decision criteria | Overview | What will they judge a solution on? |
| Decision process | Reinforce | What are the literal steps to a yes? |
| Paper process | Reinforce | Contracts, procurement, legal review: what happens after the verbal yes? |
| Identify pain | Label | The compelling reason to act now, made concrete as the cost of doing nothing. |
| Champion | Throughout | Who inside wants this and will push it? |
| Competition | Explain | What else are they considering, including doing nothing or doing it themselves? |

For low-ticket sales only the transferable pillars matter (Metrics, Pain, Decision criteria, Competition).

## Objection bank

Every answer validates first, then reframes with proof. Never argue. Tailor to the project.

- **"We'll just do it ourselves / with a free tool."** Validate: the tools are real. Reframe: the value is the result and the system around it (the setup, the follow-through, the fit to their situation), and most people with the free tool still do not get the result. Show proof.
- **"You're new / you have no track record."** Validate, then make the strength visible: depth in the subject, attention a large provider cannot give, a price that reflects it. Offer a proof case or a guarantee.
- **"It's too expensive / not now."** Validate the budget reality. Reframe to the cost of waiting, and offer the lowest-risk entry (a guarantee, a first step) before any large spend.
- **"Will this actually work for me?"** This is Perceived Likelihood: answer with the guarantee, the method and real results. Never invent results.
- **"Does this apply to a business like ours?"** Do not shrink the offer to win the point. Show the size of the change for them, with one concrete example from their world.
- **"I can learn this free online."** Validate. Reframe: free material is scattered; the offer is a structured path to a specific outcome with accountability. Sell the destination and the speed.
- **Silence, or "let me think about it."** Validate, then surface the real unspoken objection (usually money, time or belief it will work) and answer that one. Offer a low-friction next step, not pressure.

Append new objections, and the answers that actually landed, to `learnings.md` as the user runs real calls.
