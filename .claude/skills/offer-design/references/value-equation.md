# Value Equation, Grand Slam Offer and stacking

The mechanics `/offer-design` applies, summarized from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers*. Use the structure and tune every example to the user's project.

## The Value Equation

```
            Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value  =  ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
              Time Delay   ×   Effort & Sacrifice
```

Raise the top, shrink the bottom.

| Term | What it is | Score it by asking | How to improve it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome (up) | The future state the buyer wants | Is the promised result the one they lie awake wanting, in their words rather than features? | Reframe to the deeper want. Do not inflate beyond what is believable. |
| Perceived Likelihood (up) | How sure they are it will work for them | What proof exists: results, case studies, a guarantee, a track record? | Add proof: case results, demos, a guarantee, hands-on support. Usually the weakest term for a new seller, so fix it first. |
| Time Delay (down) | How long until they feel the result | When is the first win? The full result? | Engineer a fast first win (a quick start, a week-one milestone). Show progress, not only the final payoff. |
| Effort & Sacrifice (down) | What they must do or give up | How much work, learning, risk or change does it ask of them? | Remove friction: templates, done-for-you steps, accountability. Trade their effort for yours where the margin allows. |

Find the single weakest term and fix that before polishing the others. Most offers fail on believability or speed, not on the dream.

## The Grand Slam Offer

An offer so good the right person feels silly saying no. Four pillars:

1. **A starving crowd.** A segment with sharp, urgent pain and the means and willingness to pay. The market matters more than the copy. Rank candidate segments by pain intensity × purchasing power × ease of reach.
2. **A value-stacked promise.** The Value Equation pushed as far as it honestly goes.
3. **A premium price,** set by value, not by the hour or the competitor (`pricing-and-guarantees.md`).
4. **Risk reversal.** A guarantee that makes the downside the seller's problem.

## Problem to solution stack

1. List every obstacle between the buyer and the dream outcome: each fear, friction and "yes, but...".
2. Turn each obstacle into a deliverable that removes it. (Obstacle: "I won't stay consistent." Deliverable: a weekly check-in and a progress tracker.)
3. That list of solutions is the offer stack. Now refine it.

## Refining the stack

- Give each deliverable a defensible standalone value, anchored on real competitor or market pricing. Never invent a number to pad the total.
- Prune hard. Cut anything that costs real delivery effort but adds little perceived value. A tight stack beats a bloated one.
- Present so the total value dwarfs the price: the transformation first, then the stack, then the price.
- Bonuses solve secondary objections and sit on top of the core, each with its own value (`pricing-and-guarantees.md`).

## Worked example (neutral)

A freelance bookkeeper wants to sell to independent cafe owners.

- **Starving crowd:** owners of one or two cafes who do their own books at night and dread the quarterly tax deadline.
- **Diagnosis:** the dream ("books done, no tax-deadline panic") is strong; the weakest term is Perceived Likelihood, because the bookkeeper has no cafe clients yet.
- **Fix first:** two free setup cases for cafes in exchange for a named testimonial, plus a conditional guarantee ("send receipts weekly; if a deadline is missed, that month is free").
- **Stack:** monthly bookkeeping (core), receipt-photo workflow (removes the effort obstacle), a one-page monthly cash summary (fast first win), a deadline calendar (removes the fear).
- **Outcome metric:** hours of evening admin saved per month.
- **Name options:** "Closed Books by the 5th", "The Cafe Back Office".
