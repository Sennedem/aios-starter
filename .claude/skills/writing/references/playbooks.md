# Playbooks

Read the playbook that matches the format. Each gives structure, length targets and traps. All of them work inside the non-negotiables (Pyramid, SCQA opening, no fluff, no AI tells), and the user's samples and learnings outrank them.

## email_short
Use for: email, chat messages, LinkedIn DMs, any message under about 200 words.

Structure:
- Subject line (email): concrete, under 8 words, says what it is about. Not "Quick question" or "Following up".
- First sentence is the answer or the ask.
- One or two sentences of context if needed.
- One specific next step if action is needed, never a vague "let me know".
- Sign-off: the user's first name, or the sign-off their samples use.

Traps: opening with "I hope this finds you well" or "Just checking in"; burying the ask in paragraph three; several questions in one message; "Let me know your thoughts" where a yes/no question or a proposed time would do.

Example skeleton:
> Subject: Proposal review, Thursday or Friday?
> Hi [name], I sent the proposal on Monday and would like 30 minutes to go through it with you.
> Thursday 14:00 or Friday 10:00, which works?
> [first name]

## social_post
Use for: LinkedIn posts, Instagram captions, YouTube descriptions, short-form text posts.

**LinkedIn**
- Line 1 is the hook: a concrete claim, an observation that runs against expectation, or a number. Not a question, not "Here's what I learned about X."
- Break lines for mobile, mostly one sentence per line.
- Body: 3 to 6 short lines, one story or one point. SCQA works well.
- Close with something the reader can do or think about. Avoid "What do you think?".
- No hashtags unless the user asks.
- Length: 100 to 250 words.

**Instagram caption**
- Line 1 stops the scroll with a claim, not a question.
- Body: conversational, 50 to 150 words.
- Hashtags at the bottom if the user uses them.

**YouTube description**
- The first two lines show in search: state the topic concretely.
- Body: 100 to 300 words on what the viewer will learn, not "in this video".
- Links below. Timestamps if relevant.

Traps: generic openers ("Today I want to talk about..."); begging for engagement; line breaks used for drama; hashtag stuffing.

## long_form
Use for: proposals, reports, essays, articles, blog posts, consulting deliverables.

Structure:
- Executive summary for pieces over about 800 words: 3 to 5 sentences, the whole pyramid in miniature.
- SCQA opening: one paragraph.
- Body: each section is one supporting point with a content-bearing heading. Inside each: sub-claim, evidence, implication.
- Conclusion: restate the top message, then one specific next step. Do not summarize every section.

Length guides: proposal 600 to 1,500 words (front-load the value); report 800 to 2,000; article 600 to 1,200.

For school or academic work, name the frameworks you apply (SCQA, MECE, issue tree, Pyramid Principle).

Traps: "Introduction" as the first heading; a rigid five-paragraph shape; asides that should be cut; restating the question before answering it.

Output: offer a `.docx` version if the user hands work in as Word files.

## script
Use for: short video scripts (30 to 60 seconds), longer video scripts, presentation voice-overs.

Structure, written for the ear:
- Hook in the first 3 to 7 seconds or first two sentences: a specific claim, a number, or a surprising statement. Not a greeting.
- Promise: what the viewer will know or be able to do by the end, in one sentence (skip it in very short scripts).
- Body: clear transitions; spoken signposting is fine ("First...", "The second thing...").
- Payoff: one call to action.

Spoken-word rules: short sentences, one breath each; contractions; read it aloud before delivering; spell out numbers the way they are said; mark pauses with line breaks.

Length: short video 30 to 60 seconds is about 75 to 150 words; a long video of 8 to 12 minutes is about 1,200 to 1,800 words; a voice-over of 20 to 40 seconds per slide is about 50 to 100 words.

Traps: writing for the eye (long sentences, semicolons, brackets); channel intros ("Welcome back!"); reading lists aloud instead of turning them into speech.

Output: plain text, paragraphs split by line breaks. Add a parallel [VISUAL] column only if the user edits the video themselves and asks for one.

## When formats blend
1. Find the dominant format (where it will be published).
2. Use that playbook as the base.
3. Borrow one element from the secondary format.
4. Note the hybrid in the delivery note.
