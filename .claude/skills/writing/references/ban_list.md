# Ban list

These words, patterns and habits are banned. If a draft contains one, rewrite the sentence. The user's samples and `learnings.md` outrank this list; add new entries whenever the user bans something.

## Banned words and phrases
- "delve" / "delving"
- "leverage" (as a verb meaning "use")
- "navigate" (as a metaphor: "navigate the complexities of...")
- "in today's fast-paced world" / "in today's digital age" / any "in today's X"
- "the world of [X]"
- "unlock" (as in "unlock potential", "unlock value")
- "harness" (as in "harness the power of")
- "robust"
- "seamless" / "seamlessly"
- "cutting-edge"
- "game-changer" / "game-changing"
- "revolutionize" / "revolutionary"
- "transformative"
- "elevate" (as in "elevate your business")
- "empower" / "empowering"
- "unleash"
- "tapestry"
- "realm"
- "landscape" (as a metaphor: "the marketing landscape")
- "ecosystem" (unless literally about biology or tech infrastructure)
- "synergy" / "synergistic"
- "holistic"
- "paradigm" / "paradigm shift"
- "at the end of the day"
- "moving forward" / "going forward"
- "circle back"
- "touch base"
- "crucial" / "essential" (as filler: "it's crucial to note")
- "pivotal"
- "myriad" / "plethora"
- "comprehensive"
- "vibrant"
- "bustling"
- "meticulous" / "meticulously"
- "testament" (as in "a testament to")
- "a journey" (as a metaphor for any process)

## Banned sentence patterns
- "It's not just X, it's Y."
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- "More than just X, it's Y."
- "X isn't just Y, it's Z."
- "Whether you're [A], [B], or [C]..."
- "Imagine a world where..."
- "Picture this:"
- "In a nutshell"
- "The bottom line is..."
- "At its core, [X] is about..."
- "When it comes to [X]..."
- "Let's dive in." / "Let's dive deeper."
- "I hope this email finds you well."
- "I just wanted to..."
- "I wanted to reach out..."
- "I'm writing to..."
- "Without further ado..."
- "In conclusion," / "To conclude,"
- "All in all,"
- "Overall,"
- Rhetorical questions used to open a section ("So what does this mean for your business?")
- Three-item lists that escalate dramatically ("It's fast. It's powerful. It's transformative.")
- Sentences that start with "Indeed," "Notably," or "Importantly,"

## Stylistic crutches
- **Em and en dashes.** Zero in final prose. Use a period, comma or colon, or rewrite the sentence. Exception: the user's own samples use them; then match their rate. Dashes inside code, paths, URLs, number ranges and table cells are fine.
- **Bold for emphasis.** Bold only for structure (subheadings, a term being defined), never inside flowing prose.
- **Bullet lists everywhere.** If it flows as prose, write prose. Lists are for steps, features and options.
- **Headers in short content.** A 200-word email or a social post needs no headers.
- **Tricolons.** "It's clean, simple, and effective." One adjective is usually enough, two if needed.

## Tone rules
- Never compliment the reader's question, idea or context ("Great question!").
- Never write "I'd be happy to help" or "I'm excited to...".
- Never apologize for the AI's limitations inside the writing.
- Never end with "Let me know if you have any questions!" or "Hope this helps!"
- No flattery toward the audience ("smart marketers know...").
- No false urgency ("Don't miss out!", "Limited time only!") unless the urgency is real and the user asks for it.

## Formatting tells
- No emojis as bullet markers in business writing.
- No "Pro tip:" or "Key insight:" inline labels.
- Do not open every paragraph with a one-word sentence for punch ("Why? Because...").
- No arrows in prose (fine in diagrams and step lists).

## When a banned word is the right word
Sometimes a banned word is the accurate term ("ecosystem" for a literal tech ecosystem, "robust" in a statistics paper). Use it, but first check for a more specific word: "ecosystem" may be "platform stack", "robust" may be "stable" or "resistant to X". Default to the specific word.
